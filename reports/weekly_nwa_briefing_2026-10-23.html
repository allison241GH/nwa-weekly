Section A — Top Stories of the Week

The week's most important, confirmed-fresh developments.

1. Markets and Macro

The week flipped the script on the melt-up: the S&P 500 slipped ~0.5% to ~7,624 and the Nasdaq shed ~0.7% (QQQ down to ~$703 after a ~2.9% midweek drop) as investors fled AI-spending exposure and crowded into staples, energy and utilities — while the 10-year Treasury yield plunged below 5% (to ~4.95% Thursday, its biggest one-day drop since the March 2023 banking scare) before bouncing back to ~5.0% Friday, with the Fed widely expected to hold at 3.75–4.0% on Oct 28. In short, money rotated out of the AI trade and into safety, and bonds caught a hard bid on growth jitters.

Why it matters to Jamie: This is precisely the choppy, risk-off regime where your income overlay earns its keep — JEPQ/JEPI's covered-call premiums cushion the drawdown that a straight QQQ position just absorbs, and the volatility spike means richer premium to harvest; meanwhile the yield dropping below 5% is a gentle nudge that the window to lock treasury money at ~5% won't stay open forever, so this is a week to collect income rather than chase the bounce.

2. Tenstorrent Raises $1.5B at $6.2B — Private AI Compute Keeps Printing Through the Selloff

Tenstorrent — Jim Keller's Toronto-based AI-chip company — closed a $1.5B Series E at a $6.2B post-money valuation (Oct 19), led by Trace Capital and Innovation X with ARK Invest, Fidelity, T. Rowe Price, Morgan Stanley, Sandisk, and Tether aboard. The money scales its Galaxy AI servers and open RISC-V silicon, and funds continued development of open-weight models — a bet on being the open, licensable alternative to Nvidia's closed stack.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel investor, the signal is the divergence — public AI names sold off hard this week while a pre-scale chip company raised $1.5B at a premium, which tells you private conviction capital is still concentrating in the compute layer even as public sentiment wobbles. The through-line for your diligence: the durable bets this cycle are on open, licensable infrastructure that doesn't depend on one vendor's roadmap. unite.ai

Talking point: When the same week brings an AI-stock rout and a $1.5B private chip round, the lesson is that "the AI bubble" is not one trade — the public multiple can compress while the private infrastructure thesis keeps compounding.

3. Anthropic Opens Claude Duet — the Proactive Agent That Keeps Working When You Walk Away

Anthropic opened a research preview of Claude Duet (Oct 21), a standalone app where Claude runs as a proactive agent: it sends a short morning brief across your connected apps, keeps working behind the scenes in its own cloud VM with a browser, and checks in before acting on anything sensitive. Built on Claude Opus 5.5, it's Anthropic's direct answer to OpenAI's "Dots" and Meta's "Muse," and is live for Pro/Max subscribers in the US.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an Applied AI leader who builds and teaches with Claude, this is the shift from reactive chat to ambient agent worth getting hands-on with now — the "it keeps going after you close the laptop" model is exactly the workflow pattern you demo, and Duet's human-in-the-loop approval classifier is a teachable governance primitive for your FAU course. SiliconANGLE

Talking point: The three frontier labs all shipped proactive agents in the same month — the product frontier has moved from "answer my question" to "manage my attention," and the winner will be whoever earns enough trust to act unsupervised.

4. a16z Rebrands Its Crypto Arm "a16z Frontier" and Points It at AI

Andreessen Horowitz is renaming its crypto unit "a16z Frontier" and broadening its mandate from blockchain into AI, autonomous machines, and the networks connecting people and AI agents (Oct 22). The move formalizes what the firm's deployment already showed — AI now accounts for ~64% of a16z's activity, and it topped the 2026 Strebulaev-Jackson AI venture ranking.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel and NWA principal, when the largest firm re-labels an entire vertical to chase AI, it's a pricing-gravity signal — the hot names will get more expensive and more crowded, which sharpens your edge toward disciplined, sanely-priced deals in the middle rather than competing for the auctions a16z is now built to win. CoinDesk

Talking point: "Frontier" is a tell: a16z is repositioning crypto as one node in a broader autonomous-systems thesis, not a standalone bet — watch for the firm to fund the connective tissue (agent payments, machine-to-machine networks) between AI and crypto rails.

Section C — Trends to Watch

Patterns building underneath the headlines.

The market has entered the "show me the AI returns" phase

This week's rotation wasn't a crash — it was a re-pricing of patience. With hyperscalers on track for ~$780B of 2026 capex and the 10-year briefly breaking below 5% on growth fears, investors sold AI-spending exposure and asked the question that defines the next leg: when does the capex convert to returns? The bubble debate has matured from "is it a bubble" into "prove the ROI."

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel and board director, this reframes every AI pitch you see — the premium is shifting from narrative ("we use AI") to evidence (margin, retention, a workflow that measurably pays back). Portfolio CEOs who can show the payback math will keep raising; those selling the story alone are about to find the window narrower.

Vertical, applied AI is quietly out-earning the frontier hype

Beneath the froth, the durable checks this week went to AI embedded in a specific workflow — Arda's $160M Series A at $1.5B for manufacturing engineering, Otter.ai's $320M for meetings, EliseAI's recent $4B mark in housing and healthcare. The pattern: owning a vertical's data and workflow beats being another horizontal model wrapper, and investors are paying for it.

Why it matters to Jamie: For NWA diligence, this is the filter that's working — ask "whose proprietary workflow and data does this compound into?" rather than "how good is the model." The vertical operators are building the data moats that survive when the next cheaper model ships, which is exactly where a disciplined angel check still buys real ownership.

Section E — Ideas and Opportunities

What Jamie might actually act on.

[FAU / mentorship session] Teach "structured vs. unstructured AI use" as the skill that protects judgment

The research consensus has sharpened: a September IBM study found 60% of employees worry AI is eroding their skills — critical thinking most of all — and the fix is structural, not abstinence. Unstructured AI use offloads thinking; scaffolded use (verification steps, reflective prompts, rubrics, AI-literacy) protects and even sharpens it. Build a class module around the distinction: same tool, opposite cognitive outcome depending on the scaffolding.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an AI educator, this is the exact line your FAU course should own — not "AI good/bad" but "here's how to use it so it makes you sharper," which is the practical, defensible position that separates your curriculum from the hype-or-fear noise.

[Family portfolio signal] A risk-off, high-vol tape is a premium-harvesting week, not a chase

The AI-stock rotation and the bond rally fattened option premiums while capping the odds of a near-term melt-up — the condition where a disciplined covered-call overlay shines: you're paid more to rent out upside you were willing to cap anyway, and the premium cushions the next down-leg. Collect income at sensible, slightly-wider strikes rather than reach for tight-strike yield.

Why it matters to Jamie: As the family's portfolio manager, this is a live decision — see this week's Covered-Call Watch for illustrative QQQ/F strikes (note Ford's Oct 28 earnings and Nov 6 ex-div), and the companion lesson on the poor man's covered call for a capital-efficient way to run the same income play with less cash tied up.

[NWA term-sheet discussion] Use the valuation froth to pressure-test founder-friendly vs. investor-friendly terms

With a pre-product lab raising $1B at $6B this week, the gap between fundable and investable has rarely been wider — a perfect moment to run an internal NWA discussion on where you'll hold the line on terms (valuation caps, pro-rata, liquidation preference) versus where you'll stay founder-friendly to win the deal. The froth makes discipline a differentiator, not a disadvantage.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel and NWA principal, defining your term red-lines before the next hot round forces the choice is how you avoid paying froth prices on reflex — and this week's Venture Learning lesson lays out exactly which terms are worth fighting for.
