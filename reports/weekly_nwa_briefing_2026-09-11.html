Section A — Top Stories of the Week

The signal that mattered most this week, through Jamie's lens.

1. Markets and Macro

A hot August CPI print landed Thursday–Friday and flipped the Fed narrative hard: the tape now prices a ~90% chance of a rate hike at next week's Sep 16 FOMC, driving the 10-year Treasury to ~4.98% (its highest since October 2023) and the 2-year to a two-year high near 4.65%, while a Brent oil spike to a two-month high added to the inflation anxiety. Stocks still fell for the week (S&P 500 −0.8%, Nasdaq −0.7%, Dow −1.6%) despite a Friday bounce (S&P +1.0%) as bargain-hunters stepped in.

Why it matters to Jamie: A market bracing for an actual hike — not just "no cuts" — is the environment your barbell was built to weather: the treasury sleeve now locks in a near-two-year-high coupon, and in a down/choppy week like this one the JEPQ/JEPI covered-call overlay does its best work, since their premium income cushions the drawdown that the passive S&P/QQQ core takes on the chin. The one thing to respect is duration — a 10-year at ~5% pressures the long-dated treasury holdings' price even as it lifts the yield you reinvest at.

2. Cohere in Talks to Raise Up to $3B at a ~$20B Valuation — With the Canadian Government at the Table

Bloomberg reported Sept 11 that enterprise-AI company Cohere is in advanced talks to raise $2–3B at a ~$20B valuation, roughly doubling its early-2025 mark, with financing that reportedly includes the Canadian government alongside existing backers. Cohere has leaned into a "sovereign AI" positioning — models and deployments that governments and regulated enterprises can run under their own control — and this round would make it the clearest test yet of whether that thesis commands a premium.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, this is the moment "sovereign AI" stops being a slide and becomes a fundable category — capital (and here, actual state capital) is flowing to the idea that data residency, on-prem control, and national-champion positioning are a moat, which reshapes how you'd evaluate any AI company selling into governments, banks, healthcare, or defense.

Talking point: When a government becomes a cap-table participant, the diligence question changes from "can they win the market" to "who is a customer, an investor, and a regulator all at once" — a structure that can be a durable moat or a governance headache, and rarely neither. Bloomberg

3. YC's Summer 2026 Demo Day: 234 Companies, and "Industrials" Is Now the Story

Y Combinator's Summer 2026 batch presented to ~1,500 invited investors on Sept 10. The batch ran 234 companies, and the headline shift was composition: Industrials jumped to ~23% of the batch (up from ~13% the prior cohort), now the second-largest category behind B2B, with founders clustering around agents that do work, infrastructure that makes those agents safer, and software wiring AI into the physical world — energy, semiconductors, robotics, healthcare, space.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, the batch composition is the leading indicator of where seed deal flow is heading over the next 18 months, and YC just told you the frontier of "fundable" is moving from pure software toward AI-applied-to-atoms — the exact territory where a former product operator's instinct for real-world workflows is an edge, not a handicap.

Talking point: "AI is the baseline, not the thesis" — a 2026 seed pitch that leads with "we use AI" is describing table stakes; the ones worth a meeting lead with a specific industry workflow the AI now unlocks. Forbes · YC

4. Four Labs Shipped New Frontier Models in One Week — and "Model Fatigue" Set In

The week crystallized a new reality: Anthropic (Claude Fable/Mythos 5.1), OpenAI (GPT-6 "Astra"), Meta (Muse Spark 1.3), and Google (Gemini 3.8 Flash) all shipped new frontier models within days of each other, prompting a CNBC piece (Sept 6) on the industry's "model fatigue." Anthropic also rolled out Claude Managed Agents (a pre-built harness for stateful jobs that run for minutes-to-hours) and reportedly committed ~$80B to compute.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an Applied AI leader, the cadence is the strategy signal — when capability and price reset every few weeks across four labs, the durable question for anything in your practice or deal flow is "what survives the next drop," and the answer never changes: proprietary data, workflow lock-in, and distribution, never the model itself.

Talking point: "Model fatigue" is a feature for a buyer, not a bug — the faster the frontier commoditizes, the cheaper your inference gets and the more the value accrues to whoever owns the customer and the workflow. CNBC

Section C — Trends to Watch

Patterns building underneath the headlines.
The venture market is a barbell — mega-rounds at one end, down-rounds at the other, and very little in the middle

Funds over $1B have absorbed the majority of all VC raised in 2026, while roughly a fifth of rounds are down rounds and the median gap between raises has stretched past ~23 months. This week's tape — multibillion-dollar AI-infra rounds alongside a steady drip of small, disciplined seeds — is the barbell in miniature.

Why it matters to Jamie: As a board director and angel, the blended "averages" are useless for planning — you're operating at one specific end of the barbell, and the terms that matter (anti-dilution, bridge structure, runway-into-the-raise) are the ones that govern the non-AI-darling companies most of your portfolio actually lives among.

AI fluency is hardening from a "nice-to-have" into a labor-market gatekeeper

More than a third of entry-level postings now list AI skills (~3x year-over-year), AI-skilled workers command a ~56% wage premium, and a growing share of employers say they'll require "AI-free" skills assessments to check for critical-thinking atrophy — the market is simultaneously demanding AI fluency and worrying about over-reliance.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an AI educator, this is the precise tension your FAU track should own — the winning curriculum doesn't just teach students to use AI, it teaches them to keep the judgment that AI use quietly erodes, which is exactly the "AI-free assessment" the market is starting to demand.

Section E — Ideas and Opportunities

What you might actually act on.
[Family portfolio signal] A rate-hike week is a duration checkpoint, not a panic button.

With the 10-year at ~5% and a hike likely next Wednesday, the reinvestment yield on the treasury sleeve is genuinely attractive — but any long-dated bond holdings take a price hit, and it's worth confirming your ladder is short enough that you're a buyer of higher yields rather than a captive of lower-priced paper.

Why it matters to Jamie: As the family's portfolio manager, this is the week to check the treasury sleeve's duration, not just its yield — and to note that the same volatility is fattening the covered-call premium your Covered-Call Watch is built around, so the income side of the ledger is quietly getting paid more to wait.

[NWA deal-sourcing] The fundable seed shape this week is "sovereign + vertical + proprietary data."

Cato (Italian government-procurement AI), Navana.ai (voice AI for regulated Indian finance), Pixxel (sovereign earth observation) — the small, disciplined seeds closing this week share a spine: a regulated or sovereign buyer, a specific workflow, and a data asset the model can't commoditize.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, this is a concrete screen you can apply to inbound this month — "who's the regulated buyer, what's the workflow, and what data compounds" filters the AI-in-a-workflow winners from the wrappers faster than any pitch-deck read.

[FAU class + mentorship talking point] "Build vs. buy" has flipped, and it changes how founders should think about their moat.

McKinsey's 2026 data shows a growing share of enterprises now skip buying software because agentic coding tools let them build it internally — which means a startup's defensibility can no longer be "we built the feature," since a customer's own agents may build it too.

Why it matters to Jamie: As a mentor, this reframes the single most important question for any SaaS founder you advise — not "is your product good," but "why would a customer with Claude Code choose to buy this rather than build it," and if there's no answer beyond convenience, the moat isn't there.
