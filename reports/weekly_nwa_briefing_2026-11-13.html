1. Markets and Macro

After its worst week since the April 2025 tariff crash, the market snapped back — the S&P 500 rose ~2.5% to 7,561.67 and the Nasdaq added ~2.3% to 25,136.27 (its best week since August) — even as a Friday chip-led fade (Nvidia's sixth straight weekly drop, AMD −8.7%) trimmed the gains. The real story was rates: hotter-than-expected CPI/PPI pushed the 10-year Treasury yield up ~30bps to 5.15% and lifted December Fed-hike odds to ~85–90%, flipping the market's bet from "cut" to "hike."

Why it matters to Jamie: For your passive S&P/QQQ core the week's whipsaw is noise, but the rate repricing is the real variable — a December hike pressures long-duration growth more than the AI-capex scare did; and in a sharp rebound week like this one, JEPQ/JEPI's covered-call overlay caps some of the upside versus straight QQQ, the trade-off you accept for the premium income that cushions the choppy stretches. 2. Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia CEO After 33 Years — No Succession Plan in Place

Nvidia founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, president, and board member effective immediately on Nov 12, after 33 years, following allegations of extramarital affairs. CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO with no succession plan in place; the board hasn't yet selected a search firm and has reportedly approached Arm CEO René Haas about the permanent role.

Why it matters to Jamie: As a board director, this is a live case study in the single most expensive governance failure there is — no succession plan at the most systemically important company in the AI economy, plus acute founder-dependency risk that every board (including NWA portfolio boards) should now stress-test. Fortune · CNBC

Talking point: Make it your next board-meeting prompt — "If our founder-CEO vanished tomorrow, what breaks in the first 24 hours?"
