Section A — Top Stories of the Week

The signal that mattered most this week, through Jamie's lens.

1. Markets and Macro

August closed as the first up-month since May (S&P 500 +2.6%, Nasdaq +3.9%), but the first days of September gave some back — the S&P sits near 7,650 and the Nasdaq near 26,100 midweek — as renewed U.S.–Iran hostilities pushed oil and, more importantly, yields higher: the 10-year Treasury touched 4.81%, its highest since November 2023, with the 30-year above 5.2%. The equity tape is holding up remarkably well against a rising long end, which is the tell worth watching, not the daily index prints.

Why it matters to Jamie: A 20-month high in the 10-year is a real discount-rate headwind for the QQQ side of your core, and it's the exact regime where JEPQ/JEPI earn their keep — rate-anxiety-inflated implied vol makes their covered-call premium richer while the passive S&P/QQQ core absorbs the repricing; meanwhile new treasury money is getting paid the best coupon in nearly two years to simply wait.

2. Nvidia Closes In on Hugging Face for ~$13B — the Biggest AI Deal of the Cycle

Nvidia is in advanced talks to acquire Hugging Face, the hub where the open-source AI world publishes its models and datasets, for a reported $12.9–14 billion (Bloomberg reported Sept 2 that a deal could be signed this week, with roughly $1B set aside as an employee retention package). Hugging Face was valued at $4.5B in 2023 and reportedly turned down a $500M Nvidia investment at a $7B valuation late last year — a decision that looks very good right now.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, this is the most useful exit comp of the year — a developer-platform company with no frontier model of its own tripled its private mark in under three years by owning distribution, which is the single clearest argument for the "picks-and-shovels above the model layer" thesis you can actually write checks into.

Talking point: Read the strategic logic honestly — Nvidia is hedging against its own best customers (OpenAI, Anthropic) building their own silicon by buying the layer that decides which open models are easiest to deploy, and on whose hardware. Bloomberg · TIME analysis

3. Anthropic Ships Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 — and Cuts the Price of Agentic Work

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 on Sept 1: same underlying model at two safeguard tiers (Mythos restricted to trusted-access programs for cybersecurity and life-sciences work), 1M-token context, 128K output, and a new state of the art on long-running agentic tasks (52.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science). The headline for builders is economic, not just capability — a 75% cut to cache-read pricing makes typical workloads ~25% cheaper than Fable 5, and highly agentic workloads up to 45% cheaper.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an Applied AI leader, the cost curve is the story — long-horizon agents that were uneconomic three months ago just became affordable, which changes what you can actually ship in your own practice and what you should expect from any "our agent runs for hours" pitch that lands in your deal flow.

Talking point: Frontier capability now improves and deflates in the same release. Any startup whose model is "we resell inference with a margin" just watched its margin structure reset for the fourth time this year. Anthropic

4. Félix Raises $200M — and Shows How Growth Rounds Are Actually Structured Now

Cross-border remittance startup Félix announced a $200M raise on Sept 1 that was only partly equity: $87M of equity led by Andreessen Horowitz plus a $113M credit facility from General Catalyst. It's a clean example of the year's dominant structuring pattern — when a business has real transaction volume, investors are increasingly happy to fund the balance sheet with debt and reserve equity for the actual company.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel and board director, this is the structure question you should be asking every capital-hungry portfolio company — every dollar of working capital funded with debt instead of equity is a dollar of dilution your early position doesn't take.

Talking point: "Could this be debt?" is now a legitimate first-round board question, not just a growth-stage one. The failure mode is founders selling equity to finance receivables. Tech Startups

Section C — Trends to Watch

Patterns building underneath the headlines.
"Seed" has quietly become a Series A

a16z led 46% of its 48 seed deals in H1 2026 at an average check of $10.5M; AI seed rounds now carry a median pre-money of ~$17.9M (42% above non-AI), and the 90th-percentile seed valuation hit $93.7M in Q1 — double 2022. Seed deal count fell 31% year over year while seed dollars rose 30%.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel writing individual checks, the entry price on the "accessible" stage has inflated past where the math works for a normal allocation — your edge is now in the rounds a16z isn't leading, which means proprietary sourcing matters far more than it did two years ago.

Enterprises are starting to build the software they used to buy

McKinsey's State of AI 2026 finds 32% of organizations have skipped purchasing at least one software product or feature because agentic coding tools let them build it internally — while the share of large enterprises scaling agents in one or more functions rose from 27% to 40%.

Why it matters to Jamie: As a board director, this is a new and underpriced risk line for every SaaS company you touch: the competitive threat isn't a rival vendor, it's your customer's own engineering team with a coding agent — ask portfolio companies what percentage of their churn is now "built it ourselves."

AI-native startups burn faster than the funding cycle assumes

Inference, API and fine-tuning costs are compressing effective seed runway for AI application companies to 12–15 months on a $2M raise, versus the ~18 months a traditional SaaS company plans around — even as investors now expect 18–24 months of runway post-raise and grade on burn multiple (net burn ÷ net new ARR) rather than raw burn.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, this is a timing trap you can diligence around in one question — a founder with 13 months of runway will be negotiating a Series A from the weakest possible position, and that dynamic, not the product, is what will set your dilution.

Section E — Ideas and Opportunities

What you might actually act on.
[FAU class + Medium post] "AI fluency is now an entry-level hiring requirement."

More than a third of entry-level job postings listed AI skills in NACE's spring 2026 report — nearly triple the prior year — and workers with real AI skills command a ~56% wage premium (67% in consulting and professional services). Meanwhile faculty intent to use AI in the U.S. and Canada fell from 76% to 67% year over year.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an AI educator, that divergence — employer demand tripling while instructor adoption declines — is the most defensible argument you have for why your FAU course exists, and it's a sharp, data-backed Medium piece nobody has written well yet.

[OuchySport feature angle] Recovery tech is shifting from measurement to intervention.

The 2026 wearable market is moving past passive tracking toward products that actively do something — recovery-specific footwear, sleep-intervention audio, and digital-twin rehab simulations that predict return-to-sport outcomes rather than just charting them.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an OuchySport co-founder, "we tell you your readiness score" is becoming table stakes; the durable product position is the intervention layer on top, and scoping that now is cheaper than retrofitting it after a competitor ships it.

[Family portfolio signal] The long end is paying you to extend, carefully.

With the 10-year at ~4.81% (highest since Nov 2023) and the 30-year above 5.2%, the treasury sleeve has its best entry coupon in nearly two years — but the same move is what's pressuring equity multiples, so this is a laddering question, not an all-in one.

Why it matters to Jamie: As the manager of the family's wealth, this is the one place this week where doing something beats doing nothing — extending a rung or two of the ladder locks in a real coupon without touching the equity core or the JEPQ/JEPI overlay.
