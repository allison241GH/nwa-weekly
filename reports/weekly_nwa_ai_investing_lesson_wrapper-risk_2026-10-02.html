Wrapper Risk

October 2, 2026 · Topic: wrapper-risk

A "wrapper" is a startup whose core product is a thin layer of UI, prompt engineering, and workflow sitting on top of someone else's foundation model — the value is mostly in the model, and the company is renting it. Wrapper risk is the diligence question that follows: if the model provider (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) decided to offer this feature natively tomorrow, or raised prices, or changed its terms, would this company still have a business? In an AI-era deal, this is no longer a clever edge-case question — it's the first thing a serious investor should ask, because the entire moat economics of the company hinge on the answer.

This matters acutely for angel and seed investors because wrappers are the most common shape of AI startup you'll see, and they demo beautifully. A slick interface on top of GPT-6 or Claude can look like a $50M company in a 10-minute pitch while having essentially zero defensibility. The job at the earliest stage is to separate the wrapper that's a feature (something a platform will absorb or a competitor will clone in a weekend) from the wrapper that's a company — one that has used the model as a wedge to accumulate something the model provider can't easily replicate: proprietary data, deep workflow integration, a distribution lock, or a regulated/trusted position. The model is the commodity; the question is what durable asset the company is compounding on top of it.

The coaching insight a seasoned investor would share: "wrapper" is not an insult, and "not a wrapper" is not a strategy — the real test is platform risk versus platform leverage. Every great AI application is technically a wrapper at the start; the winners convert the model's leverage into a moat the platform owner won't bother or be able to attack. The way you underwrite this is with the "disintermediation test": imagine the platform launches your exact feature to its full user base. If that kills the company, you're funding a feature at a company price. If the company still wins because it owns the data, the workflow, the relationship, or the compliance posture — that's an investable AI business. Ask it on every AI deal, and ask it harder the week a platform expands its surface area.

Key Terms

Wrapper — A startup whose product is primarily a lightweight interface and workflow layer on top of a third-party foundation model, with limited proprietary technology of its own. Defensibility depends entirely on what it builds around the model.

Platform Risk — The risk that the platform a startup depends on (here, the foundation-model provider) changes pricing, terms, or — most dangerously — launches the startup's core feature itself, disintermediating it. The inverse is platform leverage: using that dependency to grow faster than you otherwise could while building a separate moat.

Real-World Example

This week's OpenAI DevDay (Sept 29, 2026) was a live wrapper-risk event. OpenAI launched a ChatGPT Apps platform letting developers build full applications natively inside ChatGPT's ~1.2 billion weekly users, plus "Dots" always-on agents and an Agents API. Overnight, every AI startup whose product was essentially "ChatGPT, but with a nicer interface for task X" had to ask whether OpenAI just became both its distribution channel and its most dangerous competitor.

Contrast that with a company like Harvey (legal AI) or a vertical player like this week's Trase ($107M for a healthcare AI OS): their defensibility isn't the model — it's proprietary legal/clinical data, deep integration into regulated professional workflows, and enterprise trust relationships that OpenAI has no appetite to build. Same underlying models; completely different wrapper risk.

Jamie's Action Prompt

Take one AI company in NWA's current pipeline or portfolio and run the disintermediation test in writing: "If OpenAI or Anthropic shipped this exact capability to all their users next week, what specifically keeps this company alive?" If your honest answer is proprietary data, workflow lock-in, distribution, or a regulated/trusted moat — strong signal. If the answer is "a nicer UI" or "we did it first," you've found the wrapper risk, and it belongs at the top of the memo.
