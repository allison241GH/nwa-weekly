Section A — Top Stories of the Week

The signal that mattered most this week, through Jamie's lens.

1. Markets and Macro

The tape flipped from rate-fear to relief: a softer-than-expected September jobs report cooled the Fed-hike talk that had dogged the market, and equities grabbed it — the Nasdaq closed at a record ~30,808 (QQQ ~$753), the S&P 500 finished roughly flat-to-up on the week (~+0.2%), and the 10-year Treasury eased back toward ~5.25% (30-year ~5.63%) as markets priced the Fed staying on hold in October. The driver was macro, not earnings — weak hiring data did the work that no Fed speaker could.

Why it matters to Jamie: For the long-term passive core this is the friendliest regime of the quarter — rates off the boil while the Nasdaq makes new highs — but it's precisely the melt-up that makes JEPQ/JEPI (and your own QQQ covered calls) give up the most upside versus a straight QQQ position; treat their fat distributions as rent collected, not a reason to let the overlay cap a running market, and keep new treasury money short where ~5.25% still pays you to wait.

2. The Megaround Has Eaten the Early Stage — 40%+ of Seed/Series A Dollars Now Go to $100M+ Rounds

Crunchbase data this week put a number on a shift every angel can feel: more than 40% of all seed and Series A dollars in 2026 have gone to rounds of $100M or larger. The week's prints drove it home — Rhoda AI emerged from stealth with a $450M Series A for its robotic-intelligence platform, Nexthop AI raised a $500M Series B for AI-workload switching infrastructure, and Ricursive Intelligence announced a $300M Series A at a $4B valuation — numbers that would have been late-stage growth rounds two years ago, now stapled to companies with little or no revenue.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, this is the defining structural risk of the cycle: when "seed" can mean $450M, the traditional early-stage entry point is being colonized by mega-funds, and the small check either gets crowded out of the hot names or pays a growth-stage price for seed-stage risk. The edge moves to disciplined sourcing in the un-hyped corners and to syndicate structures that let you ride the breakouts you can access.

Talking point: The barbell is real — a handful of capital-intensive AI names vacuuming up nine-figure rounds, and everyone else fighting for normal-sized checks. Price discipline, not FOMO, is the only defense against buying seed risk at Series C math. Crunchbase News

3. OpenAI's DevDay Turns ChatGPT Into an Agent Platform — "Dots" and Native Apps for 1.2B Users

At DevDay (Sept 29) OpenAI made 20+ announcements, but two reframe the competitive map: "Dots," always-on persistent agents that each get their own cloud computer and work across connected apps while carrying context between sessions; and a ChatGPT Apps platform letting outside developers build full applications that run natively inside ChatGPT's ~1.2 billion weekly users, plus an Agents API with computer use and a new GPT-6.1 Sol model. Altman and CFO Friar also openly discussed an eventual IPO.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an Applied AI leader, this is the clearest sign the battleground is shifting from models to distribution — OpenAI is trying to become the operating system other AI products live inside, the way iOS became for apps. For anything you build (OuchySport, class demos) the strategic question is now "do I ride a platform's distribution or get disintermediated by it?"

Talking point: When the model vendor opens an app store to 1.2B users, every AI "wrapper" startup just learned whether it's a feature or a company — platform risk is now a first-order diligence item, not a footnote. OpenAI DevDay Recap

4. Anthropic Ships Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Takes Claude for Government to Full Availability

Anthropic had its own week: Claude Sonnet 5.5 shipped (Sept 28), extending the cheaper-floor 5.5 family into the workhorse tier, and Claude for Government reached general availability at FedRAMP High (Sept 30) — opening the regulated public-sector market with the compliance posture agencies require. It's a two-front move: push capability down the cost curve while expanding into the highest-trust buyers.

Why it matters to Jamie: As someone who builds and teaches on Claude, Sonnet 5.5 means the day-to-day workflows you run and demo get another step cheaper and faster; and FedRAMP-High availability is a signal worth teaching — the AI race is increasingly won on trust infrastructure (compliance, audit, data handling), not just benchmark scores.

Talking point: The frontier labs are now competing on two axes at once — a collapsing price floor and enterprise/government trust — and the second one is where durable, defensible revenue actually lives. ExecutiveBiz

Section C — Trends to Watch

Patterns building underneath the headlines.
Answer-Engine Optimization is becoming a real marketing category

With OpenAI's native apps, agents browsing on users' behalf, and tools like Yext's and ZoomInfo's new agent harnesses optimizing "AI discovery," brands are waking up to a new surface: visibility inside AI answers, not just blue links. After Profound's $180M round for "answer-engine optimization" last month, this week's agent launches confirm AEO is maturing from buzzword to budget line.

Why it matters to Jamie: As a board director and former product leader, this is the next SEO-scale shift — any portfolio company that depends on being found needs a view on how it surfaces inside ChatGPT, Gemini and agent workflows, because the discovery layer is quietly being rebuilt.

The AI IPO/liquidity window is cracking open

Altman and CFO Friar openly fielding IPO questions at DevDay, Anthropic's publicly discussed IPO filing path at a ~$965B valuation, and a string of secondaries suggest the largest AI names are edging toward public markets or structured liquidity. After a long drought, the exit environment that sets the whole venture return clock is starting to thaw at the very top.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, exits are what turn paper markups into returns — a reopening top-end IPO window eventually pulls liquidity down through late- and growth-stage secondaries, which is what ultimately makes early positions real. Worth watching as a leading indicator for the whole asset class.

AI-leveraged solo GPs and micro-funds are becoming a structural force

The same tooling that lets a solo founder run a company (agents, copilots, automated diligence) is letting individual investors run real funds — AI-augmented micro-funds and solo GPs are sourcing, screening and managing portfolios at a scale that used to require a team. The megaround barbell is pushing differentiated capital toward exactly these nimble, specialized vehicles.

Why it matters to Jamie: For NWA and his own angel practice, this is both competition and template — the leverage that makes a solo GP viable is available to him too, and it reframes how a lean investment operation can punch above its headcount.

Section E — Ideas and Opportunities

What Jamie might actually act on.
[Family portfolio signal] A record Nasdaq is the moment to re-check your covered-call strikes, not add more

With QQQ at ~$753 and the Nasdaq printing all-time highs on cooling rate fears, short calls written in a calmer tape are now far closer to the money — this is the classic melt-up where an overlay quietly caps the upside you'd most want to keep. The move is to review strikes and roll untenably-tight calls up-and-out rather than reflexively selling new ones into strength.

Why it matters to Jamie: As the family's portfolio manager, this is a live decision this week — see this week's Covered-Call Watch for illustrative QQQ/F strikes, and the companion lesson on when NOT to sell covered calls for the discipline behind it.

[OuchySport product angle] Persistent "Dots-style" agents point at always-on recovery coaching

OpenAI's always-on agents normalize the idea of a software agent that keeps working on a goal between sessions — exactly the shape a return-to-sport product wants: an agent that tracks an athlete's recovery, nudges adherence, and adapts the plan without the user re-opening an app. The platform primitives to build this just got cheaper and more native.

Why it matters to Jamie: As OuchySport's co-founder, the persistent-agent pattern is a concrete product experiment worth scoping — recovery is inherently a long-horizon, nudge-driven journey, which is precisely where an always-on agent beats a passive app.

[FAU / mentorship session] Teach "founder judgment vs. model access" in a megaround world

With $450M Series A's going to pre-revenue AI teams, the evaluation skill students and mentees most need isn't spotting the hot sector — it's separating a founder's durable judgment from their temporary access to a frontier model anyone can now rent. Build a session around how to tell the two apart in a pitch.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an educator and mentor, this is a timely, high-leverage teaching module — it arms the next cohort with the exact diligence instinct this cycle punishes people for lacking, and draws directly on deals you can reference from this week's news.
