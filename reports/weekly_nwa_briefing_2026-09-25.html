Section A — Top Stories of the Week

The signal that mattered most this week, through Jamie's lens.

1. Markets and Macro

US equities ground higher even as the bond market buckled: the S&P 500 closed the week near 7,743 and the Nasdaq around 27,069, shrugging off a sharp Treasury selloff that drove the 10-year yield to roughly 5.18% — its highest since the financial crisis, with the 30-year near 5.48% — helped by oil sliding toward $92 on hopes of easing Gulf tensions. The move was all about rates, not earnings: fresh off the Sept 16 hike, New York Fed President Williams called another hike by year-end "reasonable" and Chair Warsh again flagged inflation as too high, pushing market-implied odds of an October hike toward ~70%.

Why it matters to Jamie: A 10-year at 5.18% is the richest reinvestment yield in a generation for new treasury money but a fresh mark-down on existing long-dated paper — so the barbell keeps favoring the short end (buy the yield, don't sit in duration). Meanwhile a Nasdaq that keeps grinding to new highs is exactly the regime where JEPQ's covered-call overlay caps the most upside versus a passive QQQ core, even as the rate-driven volatility keeps the premiums it (and you) collect fat — treat that premium as rent, not a reason to chase.

2. Two $400M Rounds in 72 Hours Say "Securing the AI Stack" Is Its Own Category

Within three days, two companies raised $400M each on the same thesis — that defending and governing the AI stack is a venture category in its own right. Cyera raised a $400M Series G extension at a $12B+ valuation (Sept 22, from Goldman Sachs Alternatives), pushing its 2026 fundraising past ~$1.4B; two days later Island raised a $400M Series F at a $6.4B valuation (Sept 24, led by Evolution Equity Partners) for its enterprise browser and AI-governance layer, more than doubling its 2024 mark.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, the fastest-compounding sub-sector this cycle isn't the models — it's the control plane around them: data security, governance, audit, and the enterprise browser as the place AI actually gets used. When two firms clear $400M in 72 hours on that single thesis, "who secures and instruments the AI a company deploys" earns its own line in diligence, not a footnote.

Talking point: Every enterprise AI rollout creates a matching security-and-governance spend — call it the "AI adoption tax." The Cyera/Island prints suggest that tax is already big enough to fund multiple $6–12B companies at once. Crunchbase News

3. Anthropic Ships Claude Opus 5.5 — Near-Frontier Capability at ~40% Less

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 (Sept 22), the first model in a new 5.5 family: it matches the frontier Fable 5.1 on most work at roughly 40% lower run-cost, with pricing dropping to $4 / $20 per million tokens and output ~30% faster, while topping agentic benchmarks (Terminal-Bench, OSWorld). It is the clearest sign yet that the competitive action has moved from a rising capability ceiling to a collapsing capability floor.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an Applied AI leader, the story of 2026 isn't a smarter ceiling — it's a cheaper floor. Near-frontier capability at 40% less means the workflows you build and teach get cheaper to run every quarter, and the unit economics of any agent you prototype (for OuchySport, for a class demo) just improved without you touching a line of code.

Talking point: When "good enough" capability drops 40% in price on a single release, the moat question flips from "whose model is best" to "who owns the workflow and data the model plugs into" — because the model itself is deflating toward commodity. ClaudeLog

4. Figure's Robots Go 9% → 56% on Chores — From Data, Not a New Design

Figure unveiled Helix 2.5 (Sept 17; Forbes write-up Sept 21) and reported its robots completed household tasks in 30 previously-unseen homes at a 56% zero-shot success rate — a 6x jump from 9% — with the entire gain traced to one change: training on "Index," its ~$1B crowdsourced dataset of real human activity, on the same robot and architecture.

Why it matters to Jamie: As a former product CPO with atoms-and-software instincts, this is the cleanest proof yet that in embodied AI the bottleneck is data, not cleverness — the lesson that reshaped self-driving now reshaping humanoids, and a direct read on where durable value accrues (proprietary interaction data) for any physical-AI deal or robotics-adjacent OuchySport angle.

Talking point: One variable changed — the dataset — and success went 9% → 56%. That's the robotics version of a scaling law: the winners will be whoever compounds real-world interaction data fastest, not whoever ships the cleverest controller. Forbes

Section C — Trends to Watch

Patterns building underneath the headlines.
Agents are becoming participants, not features

Two launches this week point the same way: Ando exited stealth (with ~$20M) as a team-messaging app where AI agents are first-class participants in the conversation, and Strada shipped tooling that lets agents record and replay workflows inside legacy portals with full audit logs. The interface for AI work is shifting from "go open the chatbot" to "the agent is already in the room."

Why it matters to Jamie: As an Applied AI leader, this is a prototyping cue — an agent that lives inside a team's workflow (a Slack-style participant, a portal operator) changes the adoption curve versus a copilot you have to remember to visit; worth testing how a participant-agent, not a sidebar, would reshape an OuchySport or advisory process.

The moat is migrating from the model to the data and workflow around it

Figure's 6x chore leap came purely from a bigger proprietary dataset; the $400M security/governance rounds are bets on owning the enterprise's AI control plane; and last week's app-layer repricings all shared a spine of proprietary data plus workflow lock-in. As raw model capability commoditizes (see Opus 5.5's 40% price cut), durable value keeps accruing one layer up.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an angel, this hardens into the single most useful diligence reflex of the cycle — "if the frontier model got 10x better and 10x cheaper tomorrow, does this company still have a business?" A confident yes points to proprietary data, workflow lock-in, or distribution; a no is a wrapper wearing a good demo.

What Jamie might actually act on.
[Family portfolio signal] A 10-year at 5.18% is a duration checkpoint, not just a headline

With the long end at a post-GFC high and the Fed openly steering toward another hike, the move is to confirm the treasury ladder is short enough to buy these yields on new money rather than sit in lower-priced long paper — and to note the same volatility is fattening the premiums behind this week's Watch.

Why it matters to Jamie: As the family's portfolio manager, this is the week to check treasury duration, not just yield, and to recognize the melt-up-plus-hike combination is paying your Covered-Call Watch more to wait while the passive QQQ core compounds.

[Medium writing angle] "Inference is deflating ~40% a release — so where does the margin go?"

Opus 5.5's price cut and OpenAI's tiered GPT-6 line make a sharp essay: as token costs collapse, value doesn't disappear, it relocates — toward proprietary data, workflow lock-in and distribution. A clean, current frame for your builder/investor audience and a ready FAU discussion.

Why it matters to Jamie: As an AI educator and writer, this is a timely piece you're uniquely positioned to write — you see it from the operator, investor and teacher seats at once, and the news peg (a 40% cut in one release) is fresh this week.

[Board governance question] Are your portfolio companies architected for model portability?

With frontier models shipping cheaper and faster almost weekly, single-vendor lock-in is now a quiet risk — a company hard-wired to one model can't capture the next 40% price cut, and inherits that vendor's outages and policy changes.

Why it matters to Jamie: As a board director, "can we swap models without re-architecting?" belongs on the diligence checklist for any AI-dependent business — it's the difference between riding the deflation curve down and being trapped above it.
