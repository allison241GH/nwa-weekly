## Section A — Top Stories of the Week

### 1. Markets and Macro

**Markets and Macro.** U.S. stocks eked out a positive week (S&P 500 +~0.5%, Nasdaq +~0.8%) but rolled over Friday after new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh used his first Jackson Hole keynote to call inflation "too high" and name price stability the Fed's predominant focus — a mildly hawkish read that spiked odds of a *September rate hike* to ~57% and pushed the 10-year Treasury yield up to ~4.72%. For a long-term holder this is noise around a rising-real-rate signal, not a reason to trade.

**Why it matters to Jamie:** A genuinely hawkish Fed chair is the one macro variable that can compress QQQ's multiple, so keep the passive S&P/QQQ/treasury core untouched and let the volatility work *for* you — a choppier, rate-anxious tape is exactly the regime where JEPQ/JEPI's covered-call premium earns its keep, cushioning the ride while the core rides out the repricing.

### 2. AI Is Still Eating Venture Capital — But the Checks Got Huge

AI startups pulled in roughly **$407 billion in the first half of 2026 — more than all of 2025 combined** — even as total deal count collapsed from ~8,290 (full-year 2025) to ~3,500 in H1, and OpenAI and Anthropic alone absorbed ~$217B (≈43% of all venture dollars) in Q2. This week's biggest rounds kept the pattern going: capital flowing to AI infrastructure, defense, and robotics in a handful of nine- and ten-figure checks rather than a broad spray of seed deals.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an angel, the headline number is a trap — the money is concentrating into mega-rounds an individual can't touch, which means the real opportunity is *upstream* of the giants (pre-seed/seed picks-and-shovels and vertical AI) where entry prices haven't inflated to match.
- **Talking point:** The barbell is now explicit — a few labs take almost half the capital, and everyone else fights for the other half; frame your own deal flow around where that "other half" is being *under*-served.

### 3. Salesforce and Anthropic Launch "Claudeforce"

Salesforce and Anthropic announced **Claudeforce**, an expanded strategic partnership embedding Claude directly into Salesforce's CRM, with "Salesforce in Claude" going to pilot customers now and an open beta expected in **September 2026**. It pairs the #1 enterprise AI lab with the #1 CRM — a distribution coup that puts Claude in front of Salesforce's entire installed base.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an Applied AI leader, this is the clearest signal yet that frontier models win through *enterprise distribution*, not just benchmarks — the moat is moving from model quality to which system-of-record you're wired into.
- **Talking point:** For your FAU exec class, Claudeforce is a live case study in "AI as a feature inside incumbents' moats" versus "AI as a standalone product" — the strategic question every founder in your mentorship pipeline now has to answer.

### 4. Anthropic Unveils a Standard to Let Claude Drive Robots and Lab Gear

Anthropic introduced the **Model Hardware Standard (MHS)** (reported by Bloomberg, Aug 27), a software standard that lets Claude and similar AI assistants interoperate with robots, scientific instruments, and manufacturing hardware — extending the "give the model tools" pattern from software APIs into the physical world.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** For a former FordPass CPO, this is the connective tissue between agentic AI and the factory floor — a horizontal standard, if adopted, is where durable platform value accrues (think USB for embodied AI), and it's a category worth watching for both your investing and product lenses.
- **Talking point:** Standards fights are winner-take-most; track whether OpenAI/Google counter with a rival protocol or whether MHS becomes the default — that fork determines who owns the physical-AI interface layer.

## Section C — Trends to Watch

- **The enterprise-agent ROI reality check.** Gartner projects ~40% of enterprise apps will embed task-specific AI agents by end-2026 (up from <5% in 2025), yet McKinsey finds only ~23% of organizations are actually *scaling* an agentic system while 39% are still just experimenting. The gap between "deployed" and "in production at scale" is the story.
  - **Why it matters to Jamie:** As a board director and investor, this is your due-diligence filter for the coming wave of "agentic" startups — separate the ~23% with real production traction from the 39% running pilots, because the multiples won't distinguish them but the outcomes will.
- **Capital is rotating from model training to inference.** The week's fundraising pattern confirms a shift: dollars are moving away from pure model training toward *inference* infrastructure, enterprise tooling, and vertical AI that solves costly real-world problems — the value-capture layer is maturing below the frontier labs.
  - **Why it matters to Jamie:** For an angel, "inference and application layer" is where a normal-sized check can still buy meaningful ownership — the frontier-model race is capital-out-of-reach, but the tooling that sits on top of it is not.
