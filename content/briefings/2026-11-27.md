## Section A — Top Stories of the Week

### 1. Markets and Macro

The AI trade staged a violent V-shaped snapback: the **S&P 500 rose ~5.6%** — its best week since the April 2025 tariff crash — the Nasdaq Composite jumped ~6.7% and **Nvidia surged ~19.9%** (up ~33% Monday alone), pulling QQQ back out of correction after the prior week's near-bear-market scare. The spark was the Nov 23 reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO plus a broad AI relief rally, even as the 10-year Treasury yield held near **5.0%** and odds of a December Fed *hike* stayed around 83% on sticky inflation and resilient growth.

**Why it matters to Jamie:** For your passive S&P/QQQ core this is the textbook payoff for riding through the correction instead of trading it — but note the mirror image of last week: in a one-week melt-up like this, **JEPQ/JEPI's covered-call overlay caps the rebound**, so the income ETFs that cushioned the drawdown will now lag the snapback in the passive core.

### 2. Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO — 11 Days After He Resigned

In one of the most extraordinary governance reversals in corporate history, Nvidia's board **unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang** as president, CEO, and director on **Nov 23 — just 11 days after his Nov 12 resignation**, and only days after it had reportedly rejected his offer to return. The board said it was satisfied as to his "candor" and, notably, that it **received input from senior levels of the U.S. government**; President Trump claimed he was "represented in the board meeting." Huang returns with SEC and DOJ investigations still active, and the stock jumped ~33% on the news.

**Why it matters to Jamie:** As a board director, this is a live, once-in-a-career case study in **founder dependency and board independence** — the single most systemically important company on earth just demonstrated that its governance couldn't survive two weeks without its founder, under visible political pressure and open regulatory probes.

**Talking point:** Use this as the opening case in your next FAU governance session: when does "irreplaceable founder" stop being a strength and become an un-diligenced key-man risk on the cap table?

### 3. Claude Helps Prove the Riemann Hypothesis — and the Proof Gets Formally Verified

Anthropic mathematician **Levent Alpöge, working with an unreleased Claude model, produced a formal proof of the (generalized) Riemann Hypothesis** — one of the Millennium Prize Problems, open since 1859. This week the **Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative announced they had machine-verified the proof** with "full confidence" (Nov 21), and Anthropic researcher Luke Sernau published a tighter 300,000-line version (Nov 25), down from the original ~17 million lines.

**Why it matters to Jamie:** As an Applied AI leader, this is the clearest signal yet that frontier models are crossing from *coding assistant* to *genuine research collaborator* — and the fact that the output was **formally verified in Lean** is the whole story: it's how we trust a proof no human will ever read line-by-line.

**Suggested action:** Make "AI-generated, machine-verified" your go-to FAU example for why verification infrastructure — not just the model — is where defensible value accrues in applied AI.

### 4. Sierra Doubles to a $30B Valuation in Seven Months

Bret Taylor's **Sierra** — enterprise AI customer-service agents — is closing a **$1.75B Series F at a $30B valuation**, led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed with Benchmark, Sequoia, GV, and Tiger, **roughly double the $15.8B it was worth in May** when it raised $950M. Annual revenue has passed $400M, and Taylor (who also chairs OpenAI's board) used the round to push back on AI-bubble fears.

**Why it matters to Jamie:** As an angel, Sierra is the cleanest data point of the year on **fundraising velocity** — a category-leading AI company can now re-price at 2x in under seven months on revenue, which resets what "recently raised" means when you're pacing follow-ons and pro-rata at NWA.

**Talking point:** When a founder tells you their last round is "only six months old," that's no longer a reason to wait — in AI, the valuation clock runs in months, not years.

## Section C — Trends to Watch

  - *Why it matters to Jamie:* For a former automotive CPO, this is the familiar lesson that **hardware scale is won on manufacturing, not just design** — the humanoid winners will be the ones who control precision production, and that's a very different diligence lens than "whose AI is smartest."

  - *Why it matters to Jamie:* As an angel with AI-infra-dependent exposure, treat this week's relief rally as **sentiment, not resolution** — the diligence question stays "does this business work if compute stays expensive and capital stays skeptical?"
