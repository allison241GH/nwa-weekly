## Section A — Top Stories of the Week

### 1. Markets and Macro

**Markets and Macro.** A hot August jobs report — **+162,000 payrolls against ~53,000 expected**, unemployment steady at 4.1% — landed Friday and did the opposite of what a tired bull wanted: it killed the September rate-cut case a week after new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh called inflation "too high," pushing the 10-year Treasury back to **~4.78%** and the 30-year to **~5.25%** while stocks slipped (S&P 500 ~**7,719**, −0.4%; Nasdaq ~**26,507**, −0.3%). The tape is now trading a "higher-for-longer" Fed into a ~Sep 16 FOMC, which is a discount-rate story, not an earnings one.

**Why it matters to Jamie:** A strong-economy-but-no-cuts week is precisely where your barbell is built to sit — the passive S&P/QQQ core keeps compounding on the growth print while the treasury sleeve gets paid a near-two-year-high coupon to wait, and JEPQ/JEPI quietly earn their keep because rate anxiety inflates the implied vol their covered-call overlay sells into; the one thing to watch is that with QQQ near record highs, that same overlay is capping your upside versus the naked-QQQ position on any melt-up leg.

### 2. a16z Raises a $1.1B "Machine Age" Fund — and Pushes Growth Fund V to $8.5B in the Same Week

Andreessen Horowitz did two things this week that, together, tell you where the smartest money thinks the constraint has moved. On Aug 28 it unveiled a **$1.1B "Machine Age" fund** to bankroll the *physical* buildout of AI — chips, memory, data centers, cooling, materials, robots, and the electrical/real-estate layer underneath — arguing the binding constraint on AI has shifted from model quality to physical capacity. Three days later (Aug 31) it disclosed that **Growth Fund V has reached $8.5B**, up $1.75B since its January launch, putting the growth practice past **$24B** across five vintages.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an angel, a fund's thesis is a map of where the returns are believed to be, and a16z just drew a big arrow away from software and toward atoms — the read-through for your own deal flow is that "picks-and-shovels for compute and power" is now the consensus institutional bet, which means the edge (and the better entry price) is increasingly in the *application* companies everyone is walking past to chase infrastructure.
- **Talking point:** When the best-resourced firm on Sand Hill raises a dedicated hardware/infrastructure fund, that's not a tip to follow them into $30B compute rounds you can't access — it's a signal to ask which underpriced software bets get abandoned in the stampede.

### 3. OpenAI Ships GPT-6 "Astra" — the Frontier Race Resets Again

OpenAI began a staged rollout of **GPT-6 "Astra"** on Sept 3, billing it as its "most intelligent and aligned" model with state-of-the-art results on computer use, software engineering, cybersecurity, and science, priced at **$10 / $50 per 1M tokens** and opening first to trusted organizations before Plus/Pro/Business/Enterprise "over the coming days." It lands days after Anthropic's own Fable/Mythos 5.1 refresh — the frontier is now improving on a rhythm measured in weeks, not quarters.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an Applied AI leader, the cadence *is* the story — capability and price are both resetting faster than any 18-month product roadmap, so the durable question for anything in your practice or your deal flow is "what survives the next model drop," and the honest answer is: proprietary data, workflow lock-in, and distribution, never the model itself.
- **Talking point:** Any pitch whose moat is "we're built on the best model" is describing a lease, not an asset. The winners rent the model and own the customer.

### 4. Tesla Launches the Cybercab — Robotaxi Goes Public in Austin

Tesla held an invite-only Sept 3 event unveiling the production **Cybercab** — a two-seat, no-steering-wheel, no-pedal robotaxi (nine cameras, AI4 compute) — and began limited public paid rides in Austin starting the next day, with ~45 Cybercabs in its registered Texas fleet. It arrives the same week **Waymo** turned on commercial service in San Diego, Tampa and Denver (now 14 U.S. cities, 4,000+ vehicles, 500,000+ weekly rides) and **Zoox** began supervised testing in Houston and San Diego.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As a former FordPass CPO, this is the week the autonomy race stopped being a demo and became a coverage-map land grab — the competitive variable is no longer "who can drive" but "who can scale a fleet and a market at once," which is an operations-and-capital problem far more than a perception one, and it's the exact muscle legacy OEMs like Ford have and the AV-natives are still building.
- **Talking point:** Watch unit economics, not launch events — a purpose-built two-seater with no wheel is a bet that removing the driver *and* the driver's car is the only path to a sub-$1/mile ride.

## Section C — Trends to Watch

- **Generative AI is turning out to be "seniority-biased" — and it's hollowing the junior rung.** Stanford HAI data circulating this week shows employment for software developers aged 22–25 has fallen ~20% since late 2022, while older developers at the same firms grew 6–12% — evidence that AI is substituting for early-career work and eroding the junior-to-senior expertise pipeline.
  - **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an AI educator, this is the sharpest possible framing for why your FAU track exists — the market is quietly removing the entry-level jobs where professionals used to *become* experts, so the durable teaching question is how to manufacture judgment when the apprenticeship rung is disappearing.
- **"AI as a feature" is losing to "AI in a workflow."** This week's fresh seed and Series A activity — Axle AI (insurtech), Metal (AI fundraising software for founders), a slate of YC-affiliated verticals — shows capital rotating decisively toward AI embedded in a specific industry's workflow, where proprietary data and switching costs live, and away from horizontal tools that a model upgrade can erase.
  - **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an angel, this is your defensibility screen in one line — ask a founder what breaks if the underlying model gets 10x better and cheaper next quarter; if the answer is "nothing, we get better too," they own a workflow; if it's "our advantage evaporates," they own a wrapper.
- **The two-tier funding market is hardening into a down-round problem for the other 95%.** Carta-sourced data circulating this week shows ~20% of 2025 rounds were down rounds (double the historical norm), bridge/extension rounds near 16.6% of cash raised, and the median gap between primary rounds stretched to ~23 months — even as AI mega-rounds distort the averages upward.
  - **Why it matters to Jamie:** As a board director and angel, the averages are lying to you — outside the AI winners, a lot of good companies are raising flat, down, or on structure, and that's exactly the environment where anti-dilution terms and runway-into-the-raise decide who keeps their equity, which is this week's Venture Learning lesson.

- **[Family portfolio signal] A higher-for-longer, near-highs tape is the textbook setup for the covered-call sleeve.** With QQQ pressing record highs and rate-anxiety keeping implied vol firm, the JEPQ/JEPI overlay — and your own QQQ/F call-writing — is being paid richer premium to rent out upside you may not fully want in a rate-capped melt-up.
  - **Why it matters to Jamie:** As the family's portfolio manager, this is a "let the structure work" week rather than a "reach for yield" one — this week's [Covered-Call Watch](https://nwa-weekly.vercel.app/options/watch/2026-09-04) lays out where the QQQ Sep 21 ex-div makes strike distance an assignment decision, not just an income one.
- **[FAU class + mentorship talking point] The Series A bar has quietly reset — and your students are building to the old one.** 2026 Series A data pegs the median round near $15M at a ~$79M post, with the ARR expectation jumping to **$5–10M** (up from the $1–3M bar of two years ago) and a hard split between AI/healthcare rounds above $50M and fintech/consumer at $8–14M.
  - **Why it matters to Jamie:** As a mentor, this is the number that recalibrates a founder's whole plan — a team targeting "$1M ARR then raise the A" is planning for a market that no longer exists, and telling them the real bar early is the single highest-leverage thing you can do in a mentorship session.
- **[Medium post / NWA thesis] The investable pattern this year is the AI-leveraged small team.** The verticalized winners closing seeds this week (Metal's $4.5M at a16z Speedrun; €5M and $17.5M rounds around tiny teams) point at a repeatable shape — a handful of operators using AI leverage to reach revenue that used to require 30 people — and it deserves a written thesis, not just a gut feel.
  - **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an angel and founder, "how few people can build this to $5M ARR" is becoming a real diligence axis, and writing the framework publicly both sharpens your own screen and sources the exact founders who fit it.
