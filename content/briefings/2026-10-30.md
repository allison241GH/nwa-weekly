## Section A — Top Stories of the Week

### 1. Markets and Macro

**Markets and Macro.** The AI trade cracked again: the **S&P 500** finished the week down ~0.6% and the **Nasdaq** off ~2% (QQQ fell ~3.5% on Friday alone, its worst day since June) as Intel's cash-burn warning and Amazon's ballooning AI capex plans reignited doubts that the buildout will pay back soon, while the **Fed held** at 3.75–4.0% on Oct 28 with two dissents *for* a hike and a St. Louis Fed call that rates may need to rise again. In short, the market is re-pricing AI-spending risk while the rate path tilts slightly more hawkish than the summer narrative assumed.

**Why it matters to Jamie:** This is the second straight choppy, risk-off week — exactly the regime where your income overlay earns its keep, because **JEPQ/JEPI's covered-call premiums cushion a drawdown** that a straight QQQ position just eats, and elevated volatility means richer premium to harvest; with the Fed on hold and a hike back on the table, this is a week to *collect* income and keep treasury ladders intact rather than reach for the AI-dip bounce.

### 2. Anthropic Files to Go Public — the $16B Revenue, $80B Loss S-1 That Will Define the AI IPO Era

**Anthropic filed its S-1** with the SEC (Oct 26), setting up a **November Nasdaq debut under ticker "ANT"** at a targeted valuation reported between **$1.5 trillion and $2 trillion** — up from a $965B private round in May. The prospectus shows **$16.3B of H1 2026 revenue** against an **$80.7B net loss**, founders retaining **50.1% voting control**, and framing language about ushering in an "age of miracles." It would be among the largest tech IPOs ever.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an angel investor and board director, this is the single most important valuation benchmark of the cycle — a frontier lab printing eye-watering revenue *and* eye-watering losses is the public-market referendum on whether AI unit economics work, and the multiple it clears will reset how every private AI round you see gets priced and defended. Read the S-1's cost structure the way you'd read a portfolio company's: revenue growth is real, but the path from $80B of losses to durable margin is the whole ballgame.
- **Talking point:** The S-1 lands in the same week the public tape sold off on "does AI capex pay back?" — Anthropic's roadshow *is* that question with a ticker attached, and its reception will tell you whether public investors are still buying the narrative or now demand the math.

### 3. OpenAI Ships GPT-6.1 Terra — the Model Built to Operate Your Computer, Not Just Answer You

**OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 "Terra"** (added broadly this week), a model it pitches as able to **see what's on a screen, operate applications, and finish multi-step workplace tasks** faster and cheaper than its predecessor — more than doubling its prior software-engineering benchmark score and sharply cutting factual errors in internal tests. It's a direct push into the computer-use agent category.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an Applied AI leader who builds and teaches with these tools, the frontier has moved from "chat that answers" to "agent that operates software," and GPT-6.1's cheaper, more accurate computer-use is exactly the capability that turns demos into deployable workflows — worth benchmarking head-to-head against Claude for the agentic tasks you show your FAU cohort and run inside NWA.
- **Talking point:** When the benchmark that jumped most is *software engineering* and the headline feature is *operating a computer*, the labs are telling you where the next productivity wave lands first — knowledge work that's really software work.

### 4. Dulo Raises a $130M Seed From Schmidt, Doerr and DST — Mega-Seeds Are Back for AI Infrastructure

**Dulo**, an AI engineering startup, closed a **$130M seed round** (Oct 30) backed by **Eric Schmidt, John Doerr, and DST Global** — one of the largest seeds of the year, landing the same day the public AI tape sold off hard. It headlined a $6B+ week of US venture funding spanning fintech (Kalshi), enterprise AI (Cohere), defense, and advanced manufacturing.

- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an angel and NWA principal, the signal is the *divergence* again — marquee operators are writing nine-figure seeds into AI infrastructure on the very day public AI names dropped, which tells you private conviction capital at the earliest stage hasn't flinched even as public patience thins. The caution for your diligence: a $130M "seed" prices in near-zero margin for error, and the named-investor halo is doing a lot of the underwriting.
- **Talking point:** A $130M seed is a round-label in name only — it's a growth bet dressed as a seed, and the gap between what "seed" means for a Schmidt-backed infra lab and what it means for the deals in NWA's pipeline is the whole lesson in stage discipline.

## Section C — Trends to Watch

*Patterns building underneath the headlines.*

### Agent governance is becoming its own product category

- As enterprises deploy agents from every platform, the new problem is *managing the sprawl*: **Dataiku shipped Agent Management** (GA this month) — a standalone product that discovers every AI agent a company is running regardless of who built it and measures its business value — while vendors from Meta (Muse for Small Business) to Oracle and ServiceNow race to let non-engineers build agents. The frontier is shifting from "can we build an agent" to "can we govern a hundred of them."
- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an Applied AI leader and board director, agent governance is the control layer every enterprise will need and most don't have yet — a real diligence lens (who owns the audit trail, the cost accounting, the kill switch?) and a genuine whitespace for a startup to own. The companies selling *oversight* of the agent fleet may out-earn the companies selling the agents.

### On-device and open models are quietly commoditizing the base layer

- Two under-the-radar moves this week: **Opera announced "WebAI,"** a proposed web standard for running open-weight models like Gemma **locally in the browser** (Oct 27), and **DeepSeek V4.1** continued the open-weight cadence that keeps resetting the price-performance floor. The direction of travel: capable models increasingly run cheaply, privately, and offline — eroding the premium on raw model access.
- **Why it matters to Jamie:** For NWA diligence, this is the recurring AI-era filter — if a startup's edge is access to a frontier model, open and on-device alternatives are coming for that margin; the durable value sits in proprietary data, distribution, and workflow lock-in, not the model itself. Ask every AI founder what survives when a free local model is "good enough."

### AI is starting to produce original scientific results, not just summaries

- OpenAI reported its internal models generated **novel mathematical proofs** — including the local smoothing conjecture for the wave equation and new results touching quantum field theory — and Anthropic has pointed to Claude accelerating **cancer and rare-disease research**. The signal isn't the specific proofs; it's that frontier models are crossing from *retrieving* knowledge to *creating* it in formal domains.
- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an AI educator and investor, this reframes the "AI as autocomplete" story your FAU students arrive with — and opens a real venture category: tools that compress R&D cycles in science, pharma, and materials. The investable question is which startups can harness model-generated discovery into defensible IP rather than one-off demos.

## Section D — Learn and Watch

*A trusted friend saying "you should watch this."*

- **📄 Essay/research — "Confidence Without Competence: AI-Assisted Knowledge Work."** The Microsoft–Carnegie Mellon finding keeps getting sharper: knowledge workers who trust generative AI more report doing *less* critical thinking, and developers who delegated coding to AI shipped working code but failed conceptual-understanding checks. The antidote the research points to is counterintuitive — deliberately make the AI *disagree* with you to generate friction. Essential framing for your FAU "AI for Business Leaders" module on using AI so it sharpens judgment rather than erodes it. Read it as an investor's case study in how to pressure-test an AI business — revenue quality, cost structure, and the distance to durable margin — the same questions you should bring to every NWA deal. ## Section E — Ideas and Opportunities

*What Jamie might actually act on.*

### [Deal-sourcing thesis] Hunt the AI-leveraged one-founder company before it's a crowded category

- Both **YC's latest Request for Startups** and **a16z Speedrun** are pointing at the same target: AI-native companies built by **tiny or solo founding teams**, where value moves to the places AI still struggles — messy documents, physical production, trusted networks, legal structure, human credibility. That's a concrete sourcing filter for NWA: founders using AI to do the work of a 20-person team, concentrated in the "AI-hard" niches rather than another horizontal wrapper.
- **Why it matters to Jamie:** As an angel and NWA principal, this is where early, sanely-priced ownership still exists while the mega-rounds chase infrastructure — a one-founder, AI-leveraged company in an AI-resistant niche is exactly the asymmetric, disciplined check your pipeline is built to win.
