A regular covered call requires you to own 100 shares of the stock, then sell a call against them. For QQQ at ~$703, that's about $70,300 of capital tied up per contract. The **poor man's covered call (PMCC)** is a capital-efficient way to run almost the same income play for a fraction of the cash. Instead of buying 100 shares, you buy one deep-in-the-money **LEAPS** call — a long-dated option, usually 9–24 months out — that behaves *like* owning the shares, then you sell a short-dated out-of-the-money call against it, exactly as you would in a normal covered call. The LEAPS is your "stock substitute"; the short call is the rent you collect.

Why it matters for a covered-call seller: the PMCC lets you harvest the same monthly premium while committing far less capital, which frees up cash for other positions or simply reduces how much you have at risk. If a QQQ LEAPS with an 80-delta costs ~$120 per share ($12,000 per contract) instead of $70,300 for the shares, you're controlling a similar amount of directional exposure for roughly a sixth of the money. The premium you collect from the short call, measured against that smaller capital base, can produce a *higher* percentage yield than the fully-funded version — that's the leverage working in your favor.

But here's the coaching insight a beginner misses: the PMCC is **not** a free lunch, and it breaks the "protection" half of the covered-call mandate. A real covered call owns shares outright — you can hold them forever and keep collecting dividends and premium through any drawdown. A LEAPS *expires* and pays *no dividend*, so you carry theta decay on the long leg and you must manage the trade actively: if the stock drops hard, your LEAPS loses value faster than shares would (it's leveraged), and if the stock rips above your short strike, your gains are capped by a *long call* rather than shares you actually own, which complicates assignment. The PMCC trades durability and dividends for capital efficiency — a great tool when you want income exposure without tying up cash, a poor tool when your real goal is to own the shares for the long haul and sleep through volatility.

## Key Terms

**LEAPS** — "Long-term Equity AnticiPation Securities," simply options that expire more than a year out. In a PMCC you buy a *deep-in-the-money* LEAPS call (typically 70–85 delta) so it moves nearly dollar-for-dollar with the stock, making it a cheaper stand-in for 100 shares.

**Poor Man's Covered Call (PMCC)** — formally a *diagonal debit spread*: long a deep-ITM long-dated call, short a near-dated OTM call at a higher strike. It mimics a covered call's payoff (collect premium, cap upside) using a LEAPS instead of shares, so you commit far less capital — at the cost of time decay, no dividends, and a defined lifespan.

## Worked Example

Say you want QQQ income exposure at ~$703 but don't want to park $70,300 in shares. You buy a ~14-month QQQ LEAPS call at the 600 strike (deep ITM, ~82 delta) for roughly $130/share — about **$13,000** of capital. Then, just like this week's Covered-Call Watch, you sell the 30-DTE ~725 call for ~$9/share (~$900). That $900 of premium measured against your $13,000 LEAPS is a ~6.9% return on capital for the month — versus ~1.3% static yield against the $70,300 of shares. The trade-off: your LEAPS collects no QQQ distribution, it loses a little value to time decay each week, and if QQQ rips past 725 you manage a spread rather than simply delivering shares. If QQQ sags, your LEAPS drops faster than stock would — the leverage cuts both ways.
