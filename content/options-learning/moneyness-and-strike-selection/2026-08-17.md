When you sell a covered call, the first real decision is *which strike* — and to make it you need two ideas: **moneyness** and **delta**. Moneyness just describes where the strike sits relative to the current share price. A strike *above* today's price is **out-of-the-money (OTM)**; a strike *at* the price is **at-the-money (ATM)**; a strike *below* the price is **in-the-money (ITM)**. For covered-call income, you'll almost always sell OTM calls — you collect premium while leaving some room for the stock to rise before your shares get called away.

Why this matters so much for a covered-call seller: the strike you pick is a direct trade-off between **how much premium you collect** and **how much upside you keep**. Sell a strike close to the price (near-ATM) and you get fat premium but you're likely to have your shares called away on any rally. Sell a strike far OTM and you keep lots of upside but collect only pennies. **Delta** is the number that makes this trade-off concrete: it approximates the probability your call finishes in-the-money (i.e., gets assigned). A 0.30-delta call has roughly a 30% chance of being assigned — which is why the classic income sweet spot is around **0.25–0.35 delta, or about 2–5% OTM**. That's exactly the band this week's Covered-Call Watch used for both QQQ and Ford.

The coaching insight a beginner misses: **don't chase the biggest premium — pick the strike by the outcome you can live with.** Ask yourself, *"If the stock closes above this strike and my shares get called away, am I happy with that total return?"* If yes, sell it. The if-called return already bakes in the gain to the strike *plus* the premium, so a strike you'd be glad to be assigned at is a strike you can sell with a clear conscience. The mistake is selling a juicy near-the-money call on a stock you actually want to keep, then feeling trapped when it rallies and you're forced to choose between letting go of shares you love or rolling at a loss.

## Key Terms

**Moneyness (ITM / ATM / OTM)** — Where the strike sits versus the share price. For calls: **OTM** = strike above price (what covered-call sellers usually sell), **ATM** = strike near price, **ITM** = strike below price. OTM calls carry only *time value* — no intrinsic value yet — which is the premium you're harvesting.

**Delta** — A rough measure of how much the option price moves per $1 move in the stock, and — handily — an approximation of the probability the option finishes in-the-money. A 0.30-delta call ≈ 30% chance of assignment. Lower delta = further OTM = safer from assignment but less premium.

## Worked Example

Take this week's QQQ at ~$731. The ~30-DTE **$750** strike is about **2.6% OTM** at roughly **0.32 delta** — so a ~1-in-3 chance of assignment, an illustrative ~$8.50 premium (≈1.16% static yield), and a 3.76% return if called. Step out to the **$765** strike (~4.7% OTM, ~0.28 delta) and the premium and assignment odds both drop — you keep more upside but collect less rent. Same stock, same week; the only thing you changed was *how much upside you're willing to rent out*. That single choice — expressed as a delta — is strike selection in a nutshell.

## Jamie's Action Prompt

Open your QQQ and F chains this week and, before looking at premiums, write down the *if-called return you'd be genuinely happy with* for each. Then find the strike whose delta sits in the 0.25–0.35 band and check whether its if-called return clears your number. If it does, you've selected your strike by outcome — not by the temptation of the fattest premium.
