## How to read this

These are **illustrative** covered-call candidates for Jamie's two single positions — **QQQ** and **F (Ford)** — built to serve the dual goal every covered call should: **income (the premium is rent) + a thin downside cushion, in exchange for renting out some upside (the strike).** Prices, premiums, deltas, and ex-dividend dates below are approximate and were sourced via web search on a headless run — **verify live quotes and the current option chain in your broker before trading.** Delta is used as a rough probability-of-assignment proxy. Nothing here is advice; it's a framework and a starting grid.

This is a live-decision week: the Fed's first hike in three years and a +2.6% Nasdaq melt-up mean **elevated volatility (fatter premiums) into a QQQ ex-dividend on Sept 21** — a setup that ties directly to this week's Options Learning lesson, [Rolling a Covered Call](https://nwa-weekly.vercel.app/options/learning/rolling-a-covered-call/2026-09-19).

**Income vs. protection read:** In a melt-up week, the temptation is to sell a tight strike for the fat premium — but that's exactly when a covered call caps the most upside on a core position you want to hold. The ~3.5–5% OTM strikes (≈0.27–0.30 delta) keep meaningful room for the passive core to run while still collecting elevated premium as a cushion.

**Ex-dividend flag (Sept 21):** The ex-div falls *inside* all four windows, but every candidate strike is **out-of-the-money** (above ~$715), so early-assignment risk is low — an ITM call is the danger into ex-div, and none of these start ITM. The caution is dynamic: if QQQ keeps ripping and a strike goes ITM *before* Sept 21, that call is an early-assignment candidate to capture the $0.69 — roll it **up and out** past the ex-div rather than let it sit ITM (this is the worked example in this week's lesson).

The ~$14 strike (≈0.24–0.30 delta) is the balance point: enough premium to matter, enough room that assignment on a modest rally still books a healthy if-called return above where the stock sits today.

**Ex-dividend / early-assignment flag (Nov 6–9):** Ford's early-November ex-div falls *inside the ~45 and ~60-DTE windows*. Both candidate strikes ($14.00 / $14.50) are OTM today, so no immediate risk — but if F rallies through the strike as the ex-div approaches, an ITM call becomes an early-assignment target the day before ex-div (the holder exercises to grab the $0.15). **Rule: don't let an F call sit in-the-money into the Nov ex-div** — roll it out and up beforehand, or accept you'll likely lose the shares and the dividend early. The 30-DTE (mid-Oct) legs expire *before* the ex-div and sidestep this entirely.

## This week's framing

Rank the goal, then pick the leg:

- **If the priority is keeping upside** on the QQQ core (reasonable in a melt-up you don't want to cap): favor the **~5% OTM, ~45–60 DTE QQQ** legs, and be ready to **roll up-and-out** if a strike goes ITM before Sept 21.
- **If the priority is income:** lean on **F** — the ~$14 / 45-DTE call plus the ~4.3% dividend is the richest income-per-dollar-of-risk here, just keep the strike OTM into the November ex-div.
- **If the priority is protection:** the tighter QQQ ~$730 (30-DTE) collects the most premium cushion, at the cost of the most capped upside — a fair trade only if you'd genuinely be content to see QQQ called away at $730.

The through-line this week is the ex-dividend calendar: QQQ's is imminent (Sept 21) but your OTM strikes are safe unless the melt-up pushes them ITM; F's is ~7 weeks out but sits inside your longer legs. In both cases the defensive tool is the same — roll before an ITM call meets an ex-div.
