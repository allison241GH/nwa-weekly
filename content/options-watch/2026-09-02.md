## How to read this

These are **illustrative** covered-call candidates for the two names you write calls against — QQQ and F — built from approximate, recently-observed prices, **not** a live options chain. Verify every strike, premium, and delta in your broker before acting; the numbers below are a framework for *where to look*, not a recommendation of a specific trade. Every candidate is out-of-the-money (OTM), targeting roughly **2–5% OTM** and a **~0.25–0.35 delta** (a rough probability of assignment). The goal is always the dual one: **income now + a thin downside cushion**, while renting out only the upside above your strike.

This week's backdrop: the 10-year Treasury pushed to ~4.81%, its highest since November 2023, and the tape has been choppy after August's first up-month since May. Rising long rates plus geopolitical noise generally means richer premium — good for a seller — but they also mean the downside tail is fatter than the calm-melt-up regime, so the premium you collect is doing real work as a cushion, not just as rent.

**Income vs. protection read:** The 30 DTE / 725 line is the income workhorse — fastest theta decay and the highest annualized capture — but it's also the tightest headroom in a month where QQQ only needs a 2.4% move to put you in the money. The 60 DTE / 735 buys you nearly 4% of runway for a 1.84% static yield; that's the leg to favor if you'd genuinely be annoyed to have shares called away. Note that at $708 QQQ is roughly 8 points below its late-August level — writing calls after a pullback means you're selling upside you may want back, so bias a notch further OTM than you would at a high.

**Ex-dividend flag — this is the month it matters.** QQQ's next quarterly distribution goes **ex-dividend on Sep 21, 2026 ($0.69/share)**, which sits **inside all three windows**. The distribution is small relative to the share price, so early-assignment risk is **low while your strike stays OTM** — but the rule is absolute: **never let a covered call sit in-the-money into an ex-dividend date you care about.** An ITM call can be exercised the day before ex-div by a holder capturing the dividend, and you lose both the shares and the distribution. The 725 strike is the one to watch: if QQQ rallies through it before Sep 21, roll up or close rather than carrying an ITM call into that date.

**Ex-dividend flag:** F's last regular ex-date was **Aug 11** (~$0.15 quarterly); the next regular ex-date falls **~mid-November**, which is **beyond all three windows** — the 60 DTE Oct 30 expiry clears it. The caution is the **roll**: rolling any of these into a November expiry would straddle F's ex-div and revive early-assignment risk on an ITM call. Also keep an eye out for a *supplemental* dividend announcement — Ford has declared them before, and a surprise ex-date inside your window changes the calculus overnight.

## This week's framing

The distinguishing feature of this month is the **QQQ ex-div on Sep 21**, and it argues for a specific bias. If your priority is **income**, the 30–45 DTE QQQ lines capture the richer, rate-anxiety-inflated premium — but you are then actively managing a strike into an ex-dividend date, so set a calendar reminder for Sep 18 to check moneyness. If your priority is **keeping upside or reducing management overhead**, push to the 60 DTE / 735 strike, where 3.8% of headroom makes it far less likely you're ever ITM into Sep 21 in the first place. On F, the trade is unchanged and simpler: 14.5 across all three windows, income on top of the dividend, with the only homework being to keep any roll clear of the mid-November ex-date.
