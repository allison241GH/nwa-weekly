## How to read this

These are **illustrative** covered-call candidates for the two names you write calls against — QQQ and F — built from approximate, recently-observed prices, **not** a live options chain. Verify every strike, premium, and delta in your broker before acting; the numbers below are a framework for *where to look*, not a recommendation of a specific trade. Every candidate is out-of-the-money (OTM), targeting roughly **2–5% OTM** and a **~0.25–0.35 delta** (a rough probability of assignment). The goal is always the dual one: **income now + a thin downside cushion**, while renting out only the upside above your strike.

This week's backdrop is a **low-to-moderate volatility regime** — the VIX is hovering near 16, off its 2026 lows but still below its long-run average. That matters because covered-call premium is a volatility product: when vol is calm, you get paid less to sell the same headroom, so the temptation to reach closer to the money (for a "worthwhile" premium) is exactly when the risk/reward is worst. On QQQ, near record highs, that reach is a mistake; on Ford, recall headlines have pumped single-name vol, so its premium is doing relatively more work.

**Income vs. protection read:** With QQQ pressing all-time highs and vol subdued, the 30 DTE / 735 line is the income workhorse but also the trap — a 2.4% cushion is thin when the index is at a level it has never held above, and a 1.18% static yield is not much rent for handing over the first 2.4% of a melt-up leg. The 60 DTE / 745 buys nearly 4% of headroom for a 1.88% static yield; in a low-vol, near-highs tape that is the leg to favor if you'd genuinely be annoyed to have shares called away, because it keeps you far enough OTM that the Sep 21 ex-div (below) is a non-event. Bias further OTM than the raw premium tempts you to — this is a "protect the upside" month, not a "squeeze the yield" one.

**Ex-dividend flag — still the governing date.** QQQ's next quarterly distribution goes **ex-dividend on Sep 21, 2026 (~$0.69/share)**, which sits **inside all three windows**. The distribution is tiny relative to a ~$718 share (≈0.10%), so early-assignment risk is **low while your strike stays comfortably OTM** — but the rule is absolute: **never let a covered call sit in-the-money into an ex-dividend date you care about.** An ITM call can be exercised the day before ex-div by a holder capturing the distribution, and you lose both the shares and the dividend. The 735 strike is the one to watch: if QQQ rallies through it before Sep 21, roll up or close rather than carrying an ITM call into that date.

Keep November calls comfortably OTM, or stay in the Oct 5 / Oct 16 expiries that clear the ex-date entirely. And watch for a *supplemental* dividend declaration around Q3 earnings — a surprise special dividend would drop a new ex-date into your window and change the math overnight.

## This week's framing

The two names are pulling in opposite directions this week, and the trade should respect that. On **QQQ**, the combination of record highs and a calm VIX means premium is thin and the risk is *giving up upside cheaply* — so if your priority is keeping the upside (or just reducing management overhead into the Sep 21 ex-div), push out to the 60 DTE / 745 strike and let distance do the work. If income is genuinely the priority, the 30–45 DTE lines capture more theta but put you on ex-div watch: set a calendar reminder for Sep 18 to check moneyness on any 735 call. On **F**, the setup is the mirror image — weakness has made the premium relatively rich, so the $15 strike is a reasonable "get paid to wait through the recall noise" trade, with the only real homework being to keep any November roll clear of the projected ~Nov 6 ex-date. Net: on QQQ, favor **protection/upside** (further OTM); on Ford, the **income** leg is more defensible because the vol is actually there to sell.
