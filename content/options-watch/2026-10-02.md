## How to read this

These are **illustrative** covered-call candidates for the two positions Jamie writes calls against — **QQQ** and **F (Ford)** — built from this week's approximate prices and the published dividend calendar. They are **not live quotes**: premiums and deltas are estimates to frame the decision, so verify the actual chain in your broker before acting. The goal is always the **dual mandate — income plus a thin layer of share protection**: the premium is rent you collect and a small downside cushion; the strike is how much upside you're agreeing to rent out.

The backdrop this week matters. With the **Nasdaq at an all-time high (~30,808)** and **QQQ near $753** after a soft jobs report cooled Fed-hike fears, this is a melt-up tape — the regime where an overlay gives up the most. That argues for **wider (lower-delta) strikes** that leave room to run, not reflexively tight calls into strength.

**Income vs. protection read:** Ford's ~4.8% dividend makes the static yields here genuinely attractive relative to the share price, but the dividend is also the trap. **Ex-div flag (REQUIRED):** The **Nov 6 ex-dividend date sits inside the Nov 14 and Dec 4 expiries.** The rule is plain — **never let a covered call sit in-the-money into an ex-dividend date you care about**, because an ITM call can be assigned *early* the day before ex-div so the counterparty captures your $0.15 dividend. The 13 strike is ~3% OTM today, so assignment risk is low *as long as F stays below 13* — but if Ford rallies into early November and that call goes ITM, expect early assignment and plan to roll up-and-out before Nov 6. The cleanest way to keep the dividend is the **30-DTE Oct 31 leg, which expires before the ex-div entirely.**

## This week's framing

Priority order for this tape: **keep upside first, income second.** With both underlyings strong and the Nasdaq at records, the mistake this week is writing tight calls that cap a running market for a modest premium. If the goal is pure income and you're fine parting with shares, the 45-DTE legs pay best; if the goal is to stay invested through a melt-up, lean to the wider 60-DTE strikes (QQQ 785, F 13.5) that collect rent while leaving room to run. And on Ford, let the **Nov 6 ex-div**, not the premium, drive the expiry choice — take the dividend by staying short-dated or well out-of-the-money through that date.
