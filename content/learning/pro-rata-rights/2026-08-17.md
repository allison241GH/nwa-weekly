A **pro-rata right** is the contractual option — not the obligation — to invest in a startup's *future* rounds enough to keep your ownership percentage from shrinking. If you own 2% after the seed and the company raises a Series A, your pro-rata right lets you buy enough of the A to stay at 2% instead of getting diluted down to, say, 1.3%. It is the single most important term most first-time angels underuse, because it's the only mechanism that lets you keep feeding your winners.

Here's why it matters specifically for angels and seed investors: venture returns follow a power law — a tiny number of deals return the whole fund. When you find one of those rockets, your seed check is small and your ownership gets diluted every round. Pro-rata is how you convert "I got in early on the winner" into "I own a *meaningful* piece of the winner at exit." Without it, you're a passenger; with it, you can double or triple down on exactly the companies where you have the most information and conviction. In a market like this week's — where AI mega-rounds (Anthropic's rumored $2T IPO, Lovable's $400M) are compounding value fast — the investors keeping their percentage through each up-round are the ones capturing the outsized outcomes.

The coaching insight business school won't teach you: **pro-rata is a right you have to *fund*, and the follow-on checks are bigger than the initial one.** This is where discipline separates good angels from lucky ones. Reserve capital *before* you deploy — many pros hold back 30–50% of a fund for follow-ons — so that when your best company raises a hot round, you're not forced to skip your pro-rata because you're tapped out. And beware the flip side: in a red-hot round, founders and lead VCs will sometimes try to *cut* your pro-rata to make room for a marquee name. Fight for it in writing at the seed, because you'll never get it back on better terms later.

## Key Terms

**Pro-Rata Right** — The contractual option to invest in future financing rounds to maintain your existing ownership percentage. It's a right, not a duty; you can exercise it fully, partially, or not at all.

**Super Pro-Rata / Pro-Rata Waiver** — *Super pro-rata* lets you buy *more* than your existing percentage in the next round (increasing ownership), a term strong seed funds negotiate for their best bets. A *pro-rata waiver* is when an investor gives up the right — sometimes forced in oversubscribed rounds — which permanently caps their share of future upside.

## Real-World Example

Union Square Ventures' pro-rata discipline in Coinbase is a textbook case: by reserving capital and exercising follow-on rights round after round, USV turned an early position into a stake reportedly worth billions at the 2021 direct listing — a return driven as much by *maintaining* ownership as by picking the company early. Contrast that with the countless angels who wrote the first check into a breakout, waived pro-rata to "let the big VC in," and watched their 3% become 0.4% by Series C — a great pick that produced a mediocre return.

## Jamie's Action Prompt

Pull up your two or three strongest angel positions. For each, answer in writing: (1) Do I actually hold a pro-rata right? (2) If the company raised a hot up-round in the next 90 days, exactly how much would my pro-rata check be — and do I have that capital reserved *today*? If the answer to (2) is "I'd have to scramble," decide your follow-on reserve number now, before the round forces the decision for you.
