Every deal term you've ever learned was calibrated for companies that take years to grow. AI has broken that assumption. When a startup can go from launch to $100M ARR in under a year — and when a frontier lab like Anthropic can post $16.3B in half-year revenue on its way to the largest IPO in history — the terms that protect your ownership are no longer the valuation and the liquidation preference. They're the terms that govern *what happens when the company 10x's before your next chance to invest.* On an explosive growth curve, the most expensive mistake isn't overpaying at entry; it's getting diluted out of the deal that actually made the fund.

Why this matters for an angel specifically: on a normal curve, your initial check is most of your return. On an unprecedented curve, your initial check is a *call option on the follow-on*, and the follow-on is where the real money is made — but it's also where you get squeezed, because when a company is tripling, every later investor wants your allocation. This is why **pro-rata rights** stop being a nice-to-have and become the single term you fight hardest for. Standard pro-rata lets you maintain your percentage; on a hypergrowth AI company, even that isn't enough, because maintaining a 1% stake through three rounds of 3x markups requires enormous follow-on capital you may not have. The sophisticated move is to negotiate for *more* than pro-rata where you have conviction, and to secure information rights tight enough that you see the next round coming early enough to decide.

The coaching insight a seasoned AI-era investor would share: **on a vertical growth curve, optionality is the asset — structure for the right to keep buying, not just the right to a markup.** Most first-time angels celebrate the paper markup when their seed company raises a monster Series A. The pros are doing different math: they're asking whether they have the pro-rata rights *and the reserved capital* to defend their position, because an uncushioned markup you can't follow is just dilution with a nicer headline. In a hype cycle, also build in downside structure — this week's correction is a reminder that unprecedented *up* curves can invert, and a clean cap table with disciplined preferences protects you when the growth story stalls.

## Key Terms

**Pro-Rata Rights** — The contractual right (not obligation) to invest in future rounds to maintain your ownership percentage. On a hypergrowth AI company this is the most valuable economic term you hold, because the follow-on rounds are where concentration — and returns — actually happen.

**Super Pro-Rata (Right of First Refusal on Allocation)** — A negotiated right to take *more* than your proportional share of a future round, often ahead of new investors. Scarce and hard to win, but on a company compounding at AI speed it can be worth more than the entire initial check.

## Real-World Example

Anthropic's path to a record $81.3B IPO this week (first-day market cap near $1.5T, Amazon and Google holding ~30% between them) is the clearest case study of why early structure matters on an unprecedented curve. The investors who captured the most upside weren't necessarily the ones with the lowest entry price — they were the ones, like Amazon and Google, who structured for *continued participation* and kept deploying into each successive mega-round as the company compounded. Compare that to the broader market this week, where the Fortune data shows 40%+ of seed and Series A dollars concentrating into $100M+ rounds: the capital is chasing the steepest curves, and the investors who pre-negotiated the right to keep buying are the ones who won't be crowded out.
